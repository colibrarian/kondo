What does KONDŌ mean?

In Japanese Buddhism, KONDŌ (金堂) means "Golden Hall." It is the central and most sacred space of a Buddhist temple, where statues of the Buddha are enshrined and important rituals are performed.

The KONDŌ represents a sacred space of spiritual harmony, inner balance, and perfection.

About Fabrics

Linen and ramie were chosen as the foundation for a reason

They are alive. They breathe. They evolve with time.

Ramie

Ramie is a natural textile fibre derived from the Boehmeria nivea plant, also known as Chinese nettle or white ramie, a member of the nettle family. Ramie has been used by humans for thousands of years.

  • Strength

    Ramie is one of the strongest natural textile fibres. It is highly durable, and its strength increases when wet, unlike many other fibres that become weaker.

  • Appearance & Texture

    Ramie has a subtle silky sheen reminiscent of silk. It is naturally crisper than linen, but becomes softer with wear and washing.

  • Hygiene

    Naturally resistant to bacteria, mildew, rot, and pests, making it a durable and hygienic choice.

  • Thermoregulation

    Highly absorbent and breathable, ramie helps regulate body temperature and feels comfortable in warm and humid climates.

  • Durability

    Ramie has excellent resistance to sunlight and retains its shape well through repeated washing.

Linen

Linen is a natural textile fibre made from the stem of the flax plant. One of the oldest textile fibres in the world, linen has been used for thousands of years and remains valued for its natural strength, breathability and distinctive texture.

  • Strength

    Linen is naturally strong and durable. Its strength increases when wet, which has contributed to its long-standing reputation as a resilient textile fibre.

  • Hygiene

    Naturally resistant to bacteria and mildew, linen stays fresh with extended wear, making it a clean and hygienic choice.

  • Breathability

    Linen allows air and water vapour to circulate freely, making it feel fresh and comfortable against the skin.

  • Thermoregulation

    Linen helps regulate body temperature — keeping the body cool in warm conditions while also providing a degree of insulation in cooler environments.

Wash & Care

Care for your KONDŌ, and it will care for you

Natural fibres may change subtly with washing and wear. This is part of their character and beauty. Care for your KONDŌ, and it will care for you — season after season.

  • Wash gently at 30°C.
  • Use a mild detergent and a low spin cycle.
  • Air dry naturally and avoid high heat.
  • Iron at a moderate temperature while slightly damp.
